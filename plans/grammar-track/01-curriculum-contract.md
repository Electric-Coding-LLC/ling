# Grammar Track Curriculum Contract

[Back to the execution map](./EXECMAP.md)

## Goal

Define an honest first Grammar sequence that turns the learner's early Vocabulary into complete Japanese sentences without implying that eight stations finish Japanese grammar.

## Tasks

### Track structure

Grammar is a first-class Foundation line below Vocabulary. It begins at the `Grammar` category station and continues in one serial row:

`Statements` → `Questions` → `Possession` → `Existence` → `Verbs` → `Tense` → `Negation` → `Adjectives`

The serial shape communicates conceptual dependency: each station reuses sentence structure and particles introduced earlier. Every station remains directly accessible. The optional footer enters Grammar after `Descriptions` and returns to the map after `Adjectives`.

### Station content

| Station | Purpose | Core forms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statements | Build basic topic-comment and noun sentences. | `A は B です`, `も` |
| Questions | Turn known sentence shapes into information and yes/no questions. | `か`, `何`, `誰`, `どこ` |
| Possession | Connect nouns through possession, affiliation, and type. | `A の B` |
| Existence | Say that objects or people exist in a place. | `ある`, `いる`, `に`, `が` |
| Verbs | Build polite action sentences with their core participants and locations. | `ます`, `を`, `で`, destination `に` |
| Tense | Contrast Japanese nonpast with polite past. | `ます`, `ました` |
| Negation | Deny noun identity and negate polite verbs in nonpast and past. | `じゃないです`, `ません`, `ませんでした` |
| Adjectives | Use い-adjectives and な-adjectives as predicates and before nouns. | `大きいです`, `静かです`, `大きい N`, `静かな N` |

Each station owns three or four complete sentence cards. Sentences primarily reuse words already present in Vocabulary; a new word appears only when the grammatical contrast requires it, such as `静か` for な-adjectives. Explanations stay concise and describe what the visible form does in the example.

### Learning directions and completion

- `meaning-to-japanese`: prompt with the English meaning; reveal the complete Japanese sentence and its pattern note.
- `japanese-to-meaning`: prompt with the complete Japanese sentence; reveal the English meaning and its pattern note.
- Progress is keyed by user, Grammar sentence ID, and review direction in a dedicated table.
- Completing a station requires every scoped sentence in both directions.
- Completion means the learner marked these sentence-pattern associations as known. It does not mean the grammatical system is permanently mastered.

### Reference and review behavior

- The station reference shows the Japanese sentence, English meaning, and one restrained explanation of the target form.
- Grammar review reuses the shared Ling launcher, modal, four-second reveal, whole-card activation, swipe/buttons, typography roles, error handling, and options menu.
- Grammar cards do not fabricate audio. Audio can be added later only with complete, verified sentence assets and the shared playback treatment.
- No Rōmaji is shown; the learner has already encountered Rōmaji as its own Foundations station.

## Constraints

- Do not duplicate Vocabulary progress or treat knowing component words as knowing the sentence pattern.
- Do not turn particles into standalone memorization cards.
- Do not introduce casual verb conjugation, te-form, counters, clauses, conditionals, honorific systems, or later Grammar stations in this tranche.
- Do not imply that `Adjectives` is the end of Grammar study.
- Do not add a new flashcard interaction or typography system.

## Exit Criteria

- The eight stations have mutually distinct jobs and form a useful conceptual sequence.
- Every sentence is natural Japanese, illustrates the declared form, and has a precise English meaning.
- The two review directions and separate completion semantics are explicit.
- The map topology and optional footer sequence are explicit.
- The line's current boundary and deferred material are explicit.
